## The room sets the terms

Stardust was a co-design with Kylee Loera at the [92nd Street
Y](https://www.92ny.org). The first thing I do on any projection job is walk
the space and find out what I can actually hit — not what the drawings say I
can hit.

The useful constraint turned out to be the least interesting one: there was no
clean throw distance for a second projector, so everything had to live on a
single surface. That decision simplified the cue stack enormously and is
probably why the piece reads as coherent rather than busy.

## Surfaces are never uniform

The wall was painted flats, which is a gift for projection — flat, matte, and
light enough to hold colour. The problem was the vertical seams every four
feet.

<Figure
  src="d849a127-166e-4a5c-5c04-5554b9231200"
  alt="Detail of a painted flat wall showing vertical seams"
  caption="Seams every four feet. Blending across them cost more time than the mapping did."
  ratio="3 / 2"
/>

We compensated by keeping large flat fields off the seams and letting the
geometry cross them instead. If you are about to do this:

1. Photograph the surface under work light before you plan anything.
2. Test your darkest cue first — that is where banding shows up.
3. Budget a full session for blending, then double it.

> The mapping is the easy part. The surface is the show.

## What I would do differently

I would insist on a second projector and eat the cost of re-rigging. The
single-surface constraint produced a cleaner piece, but it was a decision made
for me in week one rather than one I chose, and that is a bad way to arrive at a
look — see the full [design portfolio](/design) for how this piece sits
alongside the rest of the work.

`cue 47` remains my favourite thing I have built for a stage.
